\begin{abstract}
Interpretability studies of reasoning models rarely have ground truth for
\emph{what the model claims to know}: probed concepts are chosen by the
experimenter, and chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness is tested at the level of
whole prose traces. We introduce a setting in which the model's claimed
knowledge is a complete, machine-verified symbolic object. Given a Rosetta
Stone linguistic puzzle---a handful of sentence pairs in an unfamiliar language
and a held-out translation question---a reasoning LLM must externalize its
analysis not as prose but as an \emph{executable mini-grammar}: a lexicon,
morphological rules, and ordering constraints in a fixed language-agnostic
format. An answer-set-programming checker \emph{certifies} the grammar by
requiring it to regenerate every context pair exactly and decode the held-out
question, returning counterexamples that drive a propose--verify--repair loop.
Each certified rule then becomes a probe target: solver-generated, certified
contrastive minimal pairs let us ask, layer by layer in an open-weight
reasoning model, where each rule is linearly decodable and where it causally
drives the answer under activation patching. This sharpens CoT faithfulness
from trace level to \emph{rule level}: is each rule the model wrote down
represented where the computation needs it, or is the written grammar post hoc?
We describe the pipeline, validate its certification core on a UKLO
puzzle---where the solver induces a unique grammar matching the official
solution and certifies all rival readings unsatisfiable---and report the
experimental design for localization on 2--3 puzzles with
DeepSeek-R1-Distill models. \TODO{finalize abstract once results land}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A central promise of interpretability is to turn what models internally encode
into knowledge that domain experts can test and validate. Doing so requires an
answer to a deceptively hard question: what, exactly, does the model claim to
know? Probing and feature-steering studies typically answer by fiat---the
experimenter picks a concept, trains a probe, and reports decodability
\citep{axbench}. CoT faithfulness studies ask whether the model's verbalized
reasoning matters causally, but operate on free-form prose traces whose
propositional content is itself uncertain \citep{lanham2023,turpin2023}.
In both cases there is no independently verified statement of the knowledge
under test.

We study a task family where such a statement can be extracted and
\emph{certified}. Rosetta Stone linguistic puzzles---the core format of the UK
Linguistics Olympiad (UKLO) and of the LINGOLY benchmark
\citep{lingoly,bozhanov2013}---present a small set of translated sentence
pairs in an unfamiliar language, followed by held-out translation questions.
They come with built-in verification: a proposed grammar of the language
either regenerates the context pairs exactly, or it does not. Human solvers
proceed by an explicit chain of contextual inferences (segment, align,
hypothesize a morpheme, test against the next pair, revise), and reasoning
LLMs verbalize superficially similar chains, yet perform far below human
solvers on these puzzles \citep{llmsolvegen,unveiling}.

We exploit the verifiability in a three-stage pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Externalize.} A reasoning LLM reads the puzzle and emits its
analysis not as prose CoT but as an executable mini-grammar: a lexicon
(form\,$\leftrightarrow$\,meaning), morphological rules, and ordering
constraints, in a fixed language-agnostic schema that compiles to answer set
programming (ASP) \citep{clingo}.
\item \textbf{Certify.} A fixed solver program checks derivability: every
context pair must be generated exactly and the held-out question must decode.
Failures return the offending pair as a counterexample, and the LLM revises in
a budget-capped propose--verify--repair loop, in the spirit of hypothesis
search \citep{wang2023hypothesis}. Acceptance is objective---the model cannot
hand-wave a rule the data contradicts.
\item \textbf{Localize.} Each rule in the certified grammar (e.g., \emph{the
verb agrees with object number}; \emph{future is marked by \texttt{na}})
becomes a probe target. Using an open-weight reasoning model, layer-wise
linear probes and activation patching over solver-generated, certified
contrastive minimal pairs answer: at which layers is rule $R$ decodable, and
where is it causally used?
\end{enumerate}

The headline question is the \textbf{faithfulness of externalized knowledge}:
does the model internally represent and use the rules it wrote down, or is the
certified grammar a post-hoc rationalization of a different computation?
Either answer is informative, and the certified grammar makes the question
precise enough to test: unlike trace-level faithfulness probes (truncation,
paraphrase, mistake injection \citep{lanham2023}), every probed claim here is
a discrete, independently testable rule that provably accounts for the data.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~A language-agnostic executable grammar format for Rosetta puzzles with an
ASP-based certifier that returns machine-checkable verdicts (certified /
counterexample / format error) and drives an LLM repair loop

(ii)~A method for compiling each certified rule into \emph{certified}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Linguistic olympiad puzzles as benchmarks.}
LINGOLY \citep{lingoly} established that frontier LLMs struggle with
olympiad-level linguistic reasoning (best model 38.7\% on hard puzzles);
LINGOLY-TOO \citep{lingolytoo} showed via orthographic obfuscation that part
of measured performance is memorization; related datasets include modeLing
\citep{modeling} and analyses of where models break down
\citep{unveiling,llmsolvegen}. These report accuracy, not mechanism. Notably,
Rosetta puzzles are pattern-based in-context learning, a regime where CoT can
\emph{hurt} \citep{curseofcot}---one motivation for asking what the verbalized
analysis actually does.

\paragraph{Program induction and hypothesis search.}
Ellis et al.~\citep{ellis2022} synthesized theories for $\sim$70 textbook
phonology problems with Bayesian program induction; hypothesis search with
LLM-proposed, programmatically verified candidates helps on ARC-style
induction \citep{wang2023hypothesis,errorsources}. We adopt the
propose--verify loop but flip its purpose: the verified program is not (only)
a way to answer the puzzle, it is the \emph{object of interpretability study}.
Faithful-by-construction pipelines \citep{lyu2023,logiclm} guarantee that the
emitted chain entails the answer; they do not ask whether the chain reflects
the model's internal computation, which is our focus.

\paragraph{CoT faithfulness and reasoning-model interpretability.}
Trace-level probes---truncation, paraphrase, mistake injection---show CoT is
often but not always load-bearing \citep{lanham2023,turpin2023}, and recent
work argues much reasoning is latent rather than verbalized
\citep{latentreasoning,thoughtology}. Feature-level studies of reasoning
models are emerging: public sparse autoencoders (SAEs) exist for DeepSeek-R1
and its distills \citep{goodfire2025,basesfeatures}, and SAE-plus-patching has
been applied to CoT faithfulness on math/QA \citep{howdoescot}. We differ in
having a certified symbolic statement of the knowledge whose representation is
being tested, and we heed the caution that simple linear baselines often match
SAEs \citep{axbench} by treating probes as primary and SAE features as a
finer-grained lens.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Executable grammar format and certification}
\label{sec:method-format}

A grammar is a JSON/YAML document in a small language-agnostic schema covering
the core of UKLO Rosetta phenomena: (i)~\emph{lexicon entries}---surface form,
meaning atoms, feature values (e.g., \texttt{taian} $\mapsto$ definite
article, \texttt{num=pl}); (ii)~\emph{concatenative word formation}---stems
plus affixes with feature constraints; (iii)~\emph{agreement
dependencies}---a feature of one constituent realized on another (e.g., verb
suffix keyed by \emph{object} number); (iv)~\emph{precedence
constraints}---a partial order over constituents (e.g., V $\prec$ O $\prec$ S).
Nothing in the schema names a language; the same format, checker, and probing
recipe apply to any puzzle in the corpus.

The document compiles mechanically to ASP facts and rules; the checker is a
fixed clingo \citep{clingo} program that tests \emph{derivability}: for every
context pair, generation from the pair's semantic frame under the submitted
grammar must produce exactly the attested sentence; the held-out question must
decode to a unique reading. The checker returns one of three verdicts:
\textbf{certified}; \textbf{counterexample} (the first failing pair, with
expected vs.\ produced form); or \textbf{format error}. The checker only
\emph{checks}---it is deliberately not an inducer---so certification is fast
and its verdicts are easy to audit. Separately, an offline solver
\emph{induction} mode enumerates all grammars consistent with the pairs, which
we use as an oracle: it tells us whether the model's certified grammar is
unique or one of several consistent alternatives, and flags puzzles whose
context genuinely underdetermines the answer (\S\ref{sec:method-faithful}).

\subsection{Extraction loop}
\label{sec:method-loop}

The model receives the puzzle (context pairs and question only) and the format
specification, and must output a grammar document; it never sees solver
internals. On a counterexample or format error, the verdict is fed back
verbatim and the model revises, up to a fixed iteration and cost budget; every
iteration is logged (prompt version, grammar, verdict). We run one frontier
closed model, as an upper bound on extraction quality, and one open-weight
reasoning model that we can subsequently probe. This yields our first research
question, \textbf{RQ-1 (extraction)}: can current reasoning LLMs express their
puzzle analysis in the format at all, how many repair iterations does
certification take, and which linguistic phenomena resist externalization?

\subsection{From certified rules to certified probe data}
\label{sec:method-localize}

A certified grammar decomposes into discrete rules $R_1,\dots,R_k$ (lexical
entries, agreement dependencies, ordering constraints). For each rule $R$ we
generate \emph{contrastive minimal pairs}: puzzle variants---a substituted
context pair, or a question whose frame differs in exactly the feature $R$
governs---such that only $R$'s applicability differs between the two sides,
with both sides re-certified by the checker. Probe datasets are therefore
generated and verified by the same machinery that verified the grammar,
rather than hand-crafted.

\textbf{RQ-2 (localization).} On the open-weight model solving the puzzle, we
train layer-wise linear probes for rule-relevant variables (e.g.,
$\texttt{obj\_num}=\texttt{pl}$) at informative token positions: the
question-final token (where the full target frame must be available before
emission---a lookahead test) and the answer token where the rule surfaces.
Controls: probes on shuffled labels, and probe-subspace ablation compared to
random-subspace ablation. We then run activation patching
clean$\to$corrupt across the certified minimal pairs and measure whether the
patched representation flips the answer token \emph{in the direction the
symbolic grammar predicts}. Where public SAEs are available for the chosen
backbone \citep{goodfire2025,llamascope}, we examine which features carry the
probe signal, as a finer-grained (but optional) lens. We ask whether rule
types stratify by depth---lexical lookup vs.\ agreement vs.\ ordering.

\subsection{Rule-level faithfulness}
\label{sec:method-faithful}

For each certified rule $R$ we record three binary properties:
\emph{written} ($R$ appears in the model's certified grammar---true by
construction for extracted rules), \emph{decodable} (some layer's probe beats
controls), and \emph{used} (patching along the probe direction changes the
answer as the grammar predicts). \textbf{RQ-3 (faithfulness)}: do
written-but-not-used rules exist---post-hoc symbolic rationalization---and
conversely does patching flip answers exactly where the grammar says it
should? We compare rules certified on the first attempt against rules that
required repair, hypothesizing that repaired rules (adopted from solver
counterexamples rather than the model's own analysis) are less likely to be
internally used. \textbf{RQ-4 (calibration)}: on puzzles the induction oracle
flags as underdetermined, the answer must come from priors rather than
context; we predict weaker context-necessity and weaker rule decodability
there. If probes and patching find \emph{no} crisp localization despite
certified behavioral knowledge, that negative result is itself a finding
about how symbolic-seeming competence is implemented.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Puzzles.} GPU probing depth is the constraint, so we study 2--3
puzzles deeply rather than sweep a catalogue: UKLO 2023 Gilbertese (Round~1,
Problem~3), plus Malay and Ndebele items chosen to be typologically distinct
(VOS order and object agreement; negation-type realization; Bantu subject
prefixes and infinitives). All three overlap a collaborator's frozen
mechanistic-interpretability corpus, enabling direct reuse of their probing
protocol and controls. As a format-leakage audit, the same schema must serve
all three without language-specific extensions.

\paragraph{Models.} Extraction: one frontier closed reasoning model and one
open-weight reasoning model---DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B or
R1-Distill-Llama-8B, the tie broken by SAE availability
\citep{goodfire2025,llamascope}. Localization runs on the open-weight model.
If the open model cannot produce certifiable grammars (the most likely failure
mode), we fall back to probing it while it solves the puzzle directly, using
the frontier model's certified grammar as ground truth; the localization story
survives, and the self-externalization claim moves to future work.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Extraction runs are logged as append-only JSONL with
frozen model identifiers, versioned prompts, temperature~0, and a hard cost
budget. Probing and patching run on single-GPU instances; activations are
streamed to object storage. \TODO{final hyperparameters, exact prompt IDs,

\section{Preliminary results}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{The certification core works end-to-end on a real puzzle
(in hand).} On UKLO 2023 Gilbertese, our ASP encoding---given only the 8
context pairs and zero language-specific knowledge---jointly induces the
grammar and decodes the held-out question \emph{E noorii taian uaa te aomata}
as ``The man saw the fruits,'' matching the official mark scheme. The induced
grammar matches the official commentary rule-for-rule
(Table~\ref{tab:gilbertese}); the answer set is unique; every rival reading of
the question (wrong number, tense, subject, or verb) is certified
unsatisfiable when forced; and leave-one-out over the 8 context pairs yields
8/8 unique correct decodes. This validates the checker semantics and gives us
regression ground truth: the extraction pipeline's checker must certify a
hand-translation of this induced grammar into the schema. It also previews
RQ-4's machinery---enumerating consistent grammars is how we certify
uniqueness or expose underdetermination.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What kind of discovery this enables.} UKLO grammars describe known
linguistics, so we claim a \emph{methodology} for certified knowledge
extraction and localization, not novel linguistic discovery. The pattern,
however, is general: whenever a domain admits an executable hypothesis format
and a checker (program synthesis benchmarks, symbolic regression, reaction
rules), the same loop turns a model's claimed knowledge into discrete,
certified, individually probeable commitments. The solver here is measurement
apparatus, not a competitor system: certification makes the extraction honest,
and the induction oracle tells us when the data could not have determined the
answer---exactly the cases where an expert should distrust a confident model.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Depth over breadth: 2--3 puzzles cannot support
claims about the distribution of faithfulness across phenomena; the schema
covers concatenative morphology and consistent ordering, excluding (for now)
fusional morphology, reduplication, and phonological alternation; probing the
model while it \emph{writes} the grammar (the strongest version of the
faithfulness claim) is harder than probing it while it solves, and we attempt
it only on the subset that certifies cleanly; and rules that are real but
distributed may defeat linear probes---which is why the patching arm, not
probe accuracy alone, carries the causal claim. Results reported here are
preliminary; all quantitative RQ-1--RQ-4 outcomes are pending and will be
reported regardless of sign. \TODO{revisit after M3/M4; add ethics/licensing
